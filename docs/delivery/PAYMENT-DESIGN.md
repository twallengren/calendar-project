# Payment-calendar evidence and boundaries

Research checkpoint: 2026-09-14. Implementation starts after the native-market gates. These are date-only operating calendars; they do not model sessions, cutoffs, TIPS/FedNow availability or instrument eligibility.

Initial evidence-led scope should be 2026–2027 for all three calendars. Use `kind: payment`, with payment-system identity rather than a market MIC. Confirm that manifest, packaging, site and query enumeration include payment calendars without requiring an exchange alias.

* **US-FEDWIRE:** the [Federal Reserve holiday table](https://www.frbservices.org/about/holiday-schedules) lists 2026–2030, with Saturday holidays leaving the preceding Friday open and Sunday holidays observed Monday. The [Fedwire operating-hours page](https://www.frbservices.org/resources/financial-services/wires/operating-hours.html) ties this table to Fedwire Funds. Crucially, the [2025-10-09 Board decision](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20251009a.htm) approves Sunday and weekday-holiday operation no earlier than 2028. Do not project the old weekend/holiday policy through 2030 merely because the general Federal Reserve table extends that far. Initial regression examples: 2026-07-03 open under the schedule, 2027-07-05 closed, 2027-06-18 open under the schedule.
* **GB-CHAPS:** the [operator's CHAPS page](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/payment-and-settlement/chaps) states weekday operation excluding England and Wales bank/public holidays. Pair this with authoritative government holiday dates. The [May 2026 consultation](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/paper/2026/cp/extending-rtgs-and-chaps-settlement-hours-next-steps) proposes additional weekend and holiday operating days no earlier than 2029; this is a proposal, not an effective calendar change. September 2027's confirmed earlier opening affects hours and does not change this date-only layer.
* **EU-TARGET:** identify this as euro T2/CLM/RTGS operating dates, not every TARGET service. The [ECB long-term closing-day decision](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2000/html/pr001214_4.en.html) lists January 1, Good Friday, Easter Monday, May 1, December 25 and December 26 in addition to weekends, effective from 2002. Cross-check current [TARGET operational documentation](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/paym/target/target2/profuse/calendar/html/index.en.html) before implementation. Do not substitute ECB staff holidays or infer substitute weekdays for weekend holidays.

Capture source-register entries and any locally retained documents with hashes. Scheduled closures can be verified against explicit operator rules/tables; exception coverage and early-close completeness require separate statements. Label model projections explicitly and mark any unresolved interval incomplete. An annual holiday list alone does not prove the absence of emergency shutdowns.

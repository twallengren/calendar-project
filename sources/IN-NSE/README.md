## Modelling decisions recorded against these sources

- **Scope: the Capital Market (equity cash) segment.** The derivatives, currency, debt and
  commodity segments have their own holiday and session circulars (some kept here as twins) and
  occasionally differ (the commodity segment trades evening sessions on some holidays).
- **The exchange's circulars are the authority, and the bhavcopy record checks them.** Each
  December the exchange notifies the next year's trading holidays (`nse-holiday-lists`); during
  the year it adds holidays and moves them "in partial modification" (`nse-additional-holidays`).
  Every holiday is dated from those circulars rather than computed, because most follow the Hindu,
  Islamic or Jain lunar calendars as the Maharashtra government gazettes them. The exchange
  publishes an end-of-day bhavcopy for every day the market traded and for no other day
  (`nse-bhavcopy-availability`); the calendar's trading days equal that record exactly for every
  date from 1 January 2010 to 14 October 2026, except the two gold-ETF-only Sundays described
  below. The research notes (`docs/delivery/NSE-RESEARCH.md`) missed four things the record
  surfaced: the 15 October 2014 election holiday, the moves of Muharram 2013 (14 to 15 November)
  and Bakri Id 2023 (28 to 29 June), and seven special live sessions of 2010-2014.
- **No weekend substitution.** Each annual circular lists the holidays falling on a Saturday or
  Sunday in a separate table and no weekday replaces them, so `weekend_shift_policy` is `NONE` and
  only weekday holidays are listed. Days whose names the circular combines (Dr. Baba Saheb
  Ambedkar Jayanti / Mahavir Jayanti on 14 April 2022; Mahatma Gandhi Jayanti / Dussehra on
  2 October 2025) are one row under the combined name.
- **Muhurat trading is a special session on a holiday.** Diwali Laxmi Pujan is a trading holiday
  in every annual list, and the exchange holds a one-hour Muhurat session that evening (or that
  afternoon in 2025), on a Sunday in 2013, 2016, 2019 and 2023 and a Saturday in 2020. Trades
  settle and a bhavcopy is published, so the day is a `SPECIAL_SESSION` beside the `CLOSED` or
  `WEEKEND` row: `is_trading_day` is true while `is_business_day` stays false. The 2026 session
  (Sunday 8 November) is announced but its timings are not yet notified: the row is PROJECTED
  with the 2019-2023 window and the EARLY_CLOSES scope (session detail) is INCOMPLETE on that
  date, so `special_sessions` refuses to answer until the timings are known.
- **Weekend sessions.** Full regular sessions on a weekend (the Union Budget days of 28 February
  2015, 1 February 2020, 1 February 2025 and Sunday 1 February 2026; Saturday 20 January 2024)
  make the day a business day through one-day weekend-policy periods (`in_nse_weekends`).
  Shortened settled sessions on Saturdays (the system-upgrade and BCP sessions of 2010-2014 and
  the two-window disaster-recovery drills of 2 March and 18 May 2024) are special sessions.
  Mock trading sessions carry no obligations and are not trading.
- **Not modelled: instrument-limited trading.** The Sunday sessions of 16 May 2010 (Akshaya
  Tritiya) and 11 November 2012 (Dhanteras) traded only gold ETFs, and the Dhanteras extended
  hours of 2011-2019 kept a few gold instruments open after the regular close
  (`nse-dhanteras-extended-hours`); none is a session of the market as a whole.
- **No early closes.** The regular session (09:15-15:30 normal market from 2010, with a pre-open
  from 18 October 2010 and a closing session after it; `nse-market-timings`) has no shortened
  form. The incidents found did not shorten a day: on 24 February 2021 trading halted at 11:40,
  resumed at 15:45 and was extended to 17:00 (`nse-outage-2021-02-24`); on 10 July 2017 trading
  halted mid-morning and closed normally. No exchange record of each day's close time was
  retrieved, so the EARLY_CLOSES scope is PROJECTED.
- **Not a closure: 21 February 2017.** The Mumbai municipal elections made it a settlement
  holiday only (`nse-settlement-2017-02-21`); the cash market traded.
- **Coverage ends 2026-12-31.** The 2027 list has not been issued; lunar holidays cannot be
  projected reliably enough to publish.
- **References.** exchange_calendars has no NSE calendar; its XBOM (BSE) calendar follows the
  same list and is the cross-validation reference. QuantLib's `India()` disagrees with the
  circulars on about eighty dates and is not used.

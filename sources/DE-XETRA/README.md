# DE-XETRA sources

Primary source: Deutsche Börse's own published "Trading calendar &lt;year&gt;" PDF, issued annually
for "the trading venues Xetra and Börse Frankfurt" (identical for both venues). Every PDF states
a single sentence of the form "there will be trading Mondays to Fridays in &lt;year&gt;, with the
exception of ..." followed by the list of non-trading weekdays for that year, and (from 2008)
names which of those dates are settlement-only days (no trading, but the securities settlement
cycle still processes). Weekend dates are never listed, so a holiday's presence or absence for a
given year can only be confirmed independently when it falls on a weekday that year.

## Modelling decisions recorded against these sources

- **Weekend holidays are never shifted.** Every PDF above states exceptions only for weekdays;
  a holiday that lands on a Saturday or Sunday is simply not listed, and there is never a
  substitute trading closure on an adjacent weekday. `weekend_shift_policy: NONE` on the
  calendar and `shift_policy: NONE` on every event source in `de_xetra_holidays` reflect this.
- **Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve are full non-trading days, not early closes.** No source
  above gives a partial/early close time for 24 or 31 December (or for any other date) - the
  exception sentence always means zero trading that day. From 2008 the PDFs separately flag
  24 and 31 December (and, in the years they are observed, Whit Monday and German Unity Day) as
  "settlement days": the securities settlement cycle still runs even though there is no trading.
  That distinction does not change whether the market is a business day for this system's
  purposes, so it is not modelled; the comment on each affected event source notes it for
  provenance instead.
- **Whit Monday and German Unity Day are exchange-discretionary, not a standing rule.** Reading
  every published trading calendar from 2003 (the oldest available) through 2026 shows neither
  holiday follows a consistent yearly pattern:
  - Whit Monday (Pentecost Monday) is a listed exception only in 2007 and 2015-2021 inclusive.
    It is absent in 2003-2006, 2008-2014, and 2022 onward (`xetra-trading-calendar-2022`
    explicitly notes 2022's Whit Monday, 6 June, was a Monday and is not in the exceptions
    list).
  - German Unity Day (3 October) is a listed exception in 2014 and 2016-2019 inclusive. It is
    absent in 2003-2013 and from 2022 onward (`xetra-trading-calendar-2022` omits 3 October, a
    Monday that year). Three years in between cannot be confirmed either way because 3 October
    fell on a weekend and the PDFs only ever list weekday exceptions: 2015 (Saturday), 2020
    (Saturday) and 2021 (Sunday). All three are omitted from
    `de_xetra_german_unity_day.yaml` rather than assumed. This makes no output difference — a
    Saturday or Sunday is already non-trading under the weekend policy — and it is also why
    neither cross-validation reference lists a German Unity Day closure for those years.
  Because neither holiday follows a computable rule, both are modelled with `explicit_dates`
  listing only the years directly confirmed as closures above, rather than a recurring rule with
  guessed `active_years`. No occurrences are projected for years after 2021 for either holiday,
  since the two most recent sourced years (2022 and 2025's antecedents through 2026) show Xetra
  trading through both.
- **Reformation Day (31 October) is a one-off closure.** 2017 was the 500th anniversary of the
  Reformation and a one-time nationwide public holiday in Germany; `xetra-trading-calendar-2017`
  lists 31 October 2017 as a settlement day alongside Whit Monday and German Unity Day. It has
  not recurred in any other sourced year and is modelled as a single `explicit_dates` entry.
- **Coverage starts 2003-01-01, not 2000-01-01.** Deutsche Börse's own trading-calendar archive
  (`xetra-cash-market-calendar-page`) does not publish a PDF earlier than 2003. Rather than guess
  at 2000-2002, `metadata.coverage.from` is narrowed to 2003-01-01.
- **`coverage.verified_through` is 2026-12-31.** `xetra-trading-calendar-2026` is the most recent
  year Deutsche Börse has published as of the 2026-09-14 retrieval date. New Year's Day, Good
  Friday, Easter Monday, Labour Day, Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, Boxing Day and New Year's Eve
  are observed without exception in every one of the 24 sourced years (2003-2026), so their
  rule-derived occurrences for 2027-2030 are left `status: CONFIRMED` (the default) rather than
  `PROJECTED`, matching how `US-NYSE` projects its own recurring closures and early closes past
  its `verified_through`. Note the distinction inside that list: New Year's Day, Labour Day,
  Christmas Day and Boxing Day are statutory German public holidays, and Good Friday/Easter
  Monday are statutory and astronomically fixed relative to Easter, so projecting them is safe
  on the law alone. **24 and 31 December are not statutory German public holidays** — they are
  ordinary working days that Deutsche Börse chooses not to trade — so their 2027-2030
  occurrences rest on an unbroken 24-year exchange practice rather than on a statute. If this
  project tightens the `verified_through` convention (CONTRIBUTING.md says everything past
  `verified_through` "should be `status: PROJECTED`", which no market pack currently does), those
  two are the DE-XETRA event sources that should be split into a confirmed range plus a
  `PROJECTED` tail. Whit Monday and German Unity Day are not projected at all past their last
  confirmed occurrence, since they are exchange-discretionary rather than rule-derived.
- **No early closes are modelled.** No sourced trading calendar states a shortened session close
  time for any date. The 2026 landing page mentions that "deviating hours may apply on December
  30, 2026" but explicitly defers the details to a future circular, so nothing is modelled for
  it; if Deutsche Börse announces a concrete early close it should be added as its own event
  source with a `close_time`, citing that circular.

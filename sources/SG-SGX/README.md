## Modelling decisions recorded against these sources

- **The exchange follows the state's list.** SGX publishes no holiday rule of its own: "SGX
  follows the Singapore holiday calendar available on the Ministry of Manpower website"
  (`sgx-trading-page`), and earlier: "There is no trading on Singapore public holidays. When a
  holiday falls on Sunday, the following Monday will be a public holiday."
  (`sgx-calendar-wb-20140821`, identical in the 2009, 2015, 2016 and 2017 captures). Each year's
  gazetted list is therefore the authority for the dates and the exchange's own tables (2013-2019
  captures, the CDP settlement-holiday lists for 2020 and 2023-2025, the live page for 2025-2027)
  and monthly trading-day counts confirm the exchange closed on them. The derivatives market
  trades on most of these days; its calendars are not used.
- **Sunday-only substitution.** A public holiday on a Sunday is followed by a holiday on the next
  weekday not already a holiday ("*The following Monday will be a public holiday." on every
  gazette; MOM 2013: "As Chinese New Year falls on Sunday 10 February 2013 and Monday 11 February
  2013, the next day, Tuesday 12 February 2013, will be a public holiday."). A Saturday holiday is
  not substituted: the exchange's counts show every weekday traded around Saturday holidays
  (May 2021: 19, December 2021: 23, June and August 2025: 21), and MOM's 2011 page explains that
  a Saturday holiday only entitles employees to a day off in lieu. `NEXT_AVAILABLE_FROM_LAST_WEEKEND_DAY`
  expresses both rules; the explicit-date holidays carry `shiftable: true` so they follow it.
- **Lunar and religious holidays are explicit dates.** Chinese New Year, Vesak Day, Hari Raya
  Puasa, Hari Raya Haji and Deepavali are listed per year from the MOM lists 2010-2027, including
  the revisions the ministry published (Deepavali 2013 to Saturday 2 November, so Monday
  4 November traded; Deepavali 2014 to 22 October; Hari Raya Puasa and Haji 2022 to 3 May and
  10 July; Vesak 2023 to Friday 2 June). Coverage ends with 2027, the last gazetted year, plus a
  projection of the fixed-date holidays to 2030.
- **One-off holidays.** SG50 (Friday 7 August 2015) and the weekday Polling Days (11 September
  2015, 10 July 2020, 1 September 2023) closed the exchange; the Saturday Polling Days (7 May and
  27 August 2011, 3 May 2025) are weekend days and bring no Monday closure.
- **Half days still exist.** SGX trades 09:00-12:30 on the eves of Chinese New Year, Christmas
  and New Year when the eve is a trading day ("Trading on the eves of Christmas, New Year and
  Chinese New Year will be from 9.00 a.m. to 12.30 p.m.", closing routine to 12:36). From
  13 November 2017 the half-day session ends at 12:00 with the closing routine to 12:06
  (`sgx-rulebook-pn13-1-rev9845`) and from 3 June 2019 a trade-at-close phase to 12:16
  (`sgx-rulebook-rn-8-2-1`); the live page lists the 2025 and 2026 half days. No SGX document
  abolishing half-day trading exists, contrary to a common belief. A weekend eve produces no half
  day and is not moved (the 2018 table marks 25 December 2018 but not 1 January 2018; 2016 and
  2023 had none). `close_time` is the end of continuous trading (12:30, then 12:00), consistent
  with the other calendars. Half days are `VERIFIED` where an SGX year table or the live page
  lists them (2014-2019, 2025-2026) and `PROJECTED` elsewhere, since only the rule survives for
  those years.
- **Unscheduled exceptions.** Thursday 14 July 2016: "the exchange ceased trading at 1138 hours,
  and remained closed for the rest of the day" (`sgx-outage-2016-07-14`): `EARLY_CLOSE` 11:38.
  Disruptions that ended in a normal or extended close carry no row: 5 November 2014
  (power-supply fault, halted 14:51, reopened 17:15 and closed 17:51, `sgx-outage-2014-11-05`)
  and 3 December 2014 (delayed open, continuous trading from 12:30, normal close,
  `sgx-outage-2014-12-03`). The exchange did not close for COVID-19 (`sgx-release-2020-04-03`).
  The removal of the lunch break (1 August 2011), its reinstatement as a 12:00-13:00 midday break
  (13 November 2017) and the trade-at-close phase (3 June 2019) are session structure, out of
  scope.
- **Reconciliation.** The exchange's Monthly Market Statistics Reports print "Number of Trading
  Days (Securities)"; every month read from those reports equals the modelled weekdays less

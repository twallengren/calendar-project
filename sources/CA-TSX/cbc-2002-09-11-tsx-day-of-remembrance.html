      TSX gains; U.S. markets falter on day of remembrance

      CBC News · Posted: Sep 11, 2002 5:02 PM EDT | Last Updated: September 11, 2002

      Trading on the Toronto Stock Exchange was light as investors did some cautious buying Wednesday. But U.S. markets couldn't hold on to their gains as the NYSE and the Nasdaq delayed the start of trading following sombre remembrance services for the victims of the Sept. 11 attacks.

      • INDEPTH: September 11: One Year Later

      The TSX which was the only North American exchange to open at its normal time of 9:30 a.m. ET saw its benchmark index gain 74.08 points to close at 6618.30.

      The TSX held a remembrance ceremony at Stock Market Place in Toronto's financial district before trading opened for the day. (The TSX does not have a trading floor.)

      TSX CEO Barbara Stymiest led a remembrance of those who died, including 24 Canadians who worked for or were visiting firms in the World Trade Center. "We are together, too, in our determination to preserve, sustain and rebuild what terror tried to take away," Stymiest said.

      "That shared determination is not something terror can destroy like another tower," she said. "It is something terror can only strengthen."

      A minute of silence followed at 8:46 a.m. ET, the time the first plane hit the first tower.

      While trading on the TSX and the TSX Venture Exchange opened at the regular time, the TSX said "the decision on whether market participants trade during that time will be left up to the individual firms."

      Another minute of silence followed at 10:29 a.m. ET the time when the second of the towers collapsed.

      The TSX said it would donate all trading fees during the first 90 minutes to the September 11th Disaster Relief Fund.

      American markets delay openings

      In New York, the NYSE and Nasdaq markets opened late to allow market participants to attend memorial services.

      The Nasdaq opened for trading at 11 a.m. ET. Trading on the NYSE began at noon ET, following the conclusion of the memorial service being held at the site of the World Trade Center.

      The Dow Jones Industrial Average ended the day down 21.44 points at 8581.17, while the Nasdaq Composite Index slipped 4.65 points to 1315.44.

      There was little in the way of company-specific news to move the markets.

      Stock exchanges overseas also observed the anniversary of the attacks against the United States. In Toyko, traders paused for a moment of silence before the session began.

      On Sept. 11 last year, the NYSE and the Nasdaq did not open for business. The TSX opened trading for 71 minutes before shutting down until Sept. 13. American markets did not reopen until Sept. 17.
